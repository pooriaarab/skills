# The surface contract

Every direction skill in this suite answers the **same twelve surfaces**, in the
same order, with the same headings. That is what makes them swappable: you can
diff two directions surface by surface and see exactly what changes.

A brand that only exists on a website is not a brand. It is a stylesheet. The
website is one of twelve places the work shows up, and it is not the one most
people meet first.

Write every rule so somebody can execute it **without asking a follow-up
question**. "Use bold type" is not a rule. "Headline at 96px, weight 800,
tracking -0.03em, flush left, never centred" is a rule.

---

## 1. Voice and writing

The register the words sit in. Cover:

- **Tone** — one sentence.
- **Casing** — headlines, buttons, and titles. State it absolutely ("all
  lowercase, always" or "sentence case, never title case").
- **Sentence rhythm** — long or short, and whether fragments are allowed.
- **Do say / don't say** — at least four each, as real phrases, not adjectives.
- **The tell** — the one habit that makes a paragraph recognisably this
  direction.

## 2. Landing page

Structure first, decoration second. Cover the hero, how sections divide, what
the page does with whitespace, and the one element that carries the direction.

## 3. X / Twitter avatar

Renders at **48px**. If the direction cannot survive 48px, say so, and give the
high-contrast export variant with exact numbers. Never claim a texture survives
that size if it does not.

## 4. X header and YouTube banner

2560×1440 for YouTube, with the **1546×423 safe area** that is all a phone shows.
Anything outside the safe area is decoration. State what sits inside it.

## 5. Open Graph card

1200×630. It renders in a feed at roughly a third of that. Give the type sizes
that survive the shrink, and what to drop.

## 6. LinkedIn banner

1584×396, and the profile photo covers the lower-left corner on desktop. State
what goes where given that hole.

## 7. LinkedIn post image

1200×627. LinkedIn's feed is the most conservative context this brand enters.
State how the direction behaves in a room full of suits — including whether it
should be dialled down, and by how much.

## 8. Instagram carousel

1080×1350 (4:5 — the tallest the feed allows, so use it). Cover **three
different slides**, because they are not the same job:

- **Cover slide** — must stop a thumb. One idea, huge.
- **Interior slide** — must be readable at speed and must look like it belongs
  to the cover.
- **End card** — the ask. State what the ask is and how it looks.

Also state the **swipe cue**: the thing on the cover's right edge that says
there is more.

## 9. YouTube thumbnail

1280×720, but design for the **~210px wide** version, because that is where the
click is won or lost. Give the rule that makes every thumbnail recognisably his
without making them all identical.

## 10. YouTube edit style

The direction has to survive motion. Cover:

- **Cut rhythm** — fast, slow, and where a cut is allowed to land.
- **Titles and lower thirds** — font, placement, in/out behaviour.
- **B-roll treatment** — grade, grain, speed.
- **Transitions** — most directions should ban all but one.
- **The cold open** — first three seconds, since that is the retention cliff.

## 11. Podcast cover

3000×3000, shown at 150px in a podcast app next to hundreds of others. Almost
every direction has to simplify here. Say how.

## 12. Deck and talks

16:9, seen from the back of a room. Cover the title slide, the section divider,
the data slide, and the one rule for slides with a lot of words.

---

## Also required in every skill

### Cost to run

Be honest. Rate the direction **cheap / moderate / expensive** and say what it
costs per asset in real terms. A direction that needs a designer for every post
is not viable for one person publishing weekly, however beautiful it is.

### Pairs with / clashes with

Link to sibling skills by relative path. Say which direction this one sits
beside in a kit, and which one it fights with, and why.

### The failure mode

Every direction fails in a specific way when done badly. Name it. This is the
most useful paragraph in the skill.
