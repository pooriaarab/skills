# Retro desktop OS windows

First used on foxlink. The whole video is a 90s desktop: every line of copy is a dialog box, and the product runs inside a window.

- **Ground:** a teal desktop `#0e7a78` with a 6 px dark dot dither (`radial-gradient`), under the scene hosts. A gray taskbar sits at y 1024 with beveled buttons (`fox`, `foxlink`, `Terminal`). Nothing drifts. The motion comes from windows that open and close.
- **Windows:** gray `#c3c3c3` with a 3 px bevel (white top-left, `#3a3a3a` bottom-right) and a 3 px hard black shadow. The 44 px title bar has a navy-to-blue gradient, the title in Silkscreen, and three beveled `_ □ ×` boxes. Each window opens with a zoom-rect: a dotted white outline steps from the taskbar to the window rect in 3 frames (`tl.set` on left/top/width/height), then the window appears. Do not fade it in.
- **Type pair:** Silkscreen 700 (Google woff2) for the headlines and the title bars. VT323 (Google woff2) for the system text, the buttons and the terminal. The main `index.html` needs its own `@font-face` for any font the taskbar uses.
- **Text entrances:** windows replace fades. A message box carries the hook (with OK and Cancel, and the cursor clicks OK). A warning box has a yellow `!` tile. A terminal window reveals the real E2E check lines one per 0.16 s (frame-stepped `tl.set`, green `#47ff6f` on black, VT323 48 px). Progress bars fill block by block (`tl.set` per block, 0.025 to 0.05 s apart).
- **Footage:** each harness still sits in a "foxlink popup" window (900 x 780). The still switches on with the window, then pushes in on the line that matters. A caption dialog opens at the right (680 x 420) with a Silkscreen headline and a VT323 sub-line. A pixel SVG cursor glides (0.45 s, `power2.inOut`) to the real Approve button, and a yellow square ring marks the click.
- **Seams:** minimize to the taskbar. A dotted ghost rect steps from the window, to half size, to a taskbar button in 3 frames before the cut. Use it on the act seams (wordmark, demo, the send proof, the proof count, the end). Every other cut is a hard cut on a beat.
- **Pacing:** shots of 6 ticks (1.45 s at 124 BPM). 14 shots in 22.5 s. No avatar.
- **Music prompt:** "Playful 1990s computer chiptune funk, 124 BPM. Square-wave lead, bouncy slap synth bass, crisp drum machine, little modem-like arpeggios. Starts on a strong downbeat with no intro, a drop at about 7 seconds where the full groove enters, upbeat to the end, clean ending on the final bar. Instrumental, no vocals." The elevenlabs/music take won: it holds steady energy at -12 LUFS momentary and ends clean at 24 s. Neither take had a real drop.
- **SFX cue map:** pop on every window open. Click on each cursor click. Glitch on the warning box. Type under every terminal reveal and under the progress bar. Whoosh into the disconnect. Riser into the proof count. Chime when the bar fills and on the end card.
- **Copy pattern:** the hook is a system dialog in the user's voice ("Your agent wants your inbox." then a warning: "Not your token."). The wordmark window carries the one-line promise and a progress bar. The step dialogs are titled by the service (`Calendar`, `Gmail`, `foxgate`, `Disconnect`), with a 3 to 5 word headline and one plain sub-line. The security claims are real terminal lines (`ok  E5: storage.local holds no token`), not paraphrase. The proof window shows `24/24` and a full bar.
